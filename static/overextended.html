How the score is built, and how to read it

The Today view scores every 5-minute bar of the session from 0 to 100 with six published intraday rules. A rule contributes nothing until it triggers and its full weight at an extreme reading. It only scores while the price is above today's open. Each card shows the peak the score reached today, when that was, and the score now. Tiers use the peak: stretched = 50+, extended = 35-49, warming up = 25-34. Over 60 days of 5-minute bars a session peaked at 35+ on about 4% of stock-days (4 names a day in the NIFTY 100, 8 in the mid/small caps) and at 50+ on about 1%.

What the data says about fading these moves. A stock far above its open is not by itself likely to close lower: in the same 60 days, after the score first crossed 35 the stock closed below that price only about 53% of the time, and the ones still pushing (2+ ATR from the open, or a score of 50+) closed higher about as often as lower, because many big moves from the open are trend days. The one version that held up in both halves of the sample: the stock reached 35+ and has then given back half or more of its run from the open. About 63% of those closed lower still, but the average move to the close was only about -0.3%, less than a round trip of costs on a small position. Those cards are marked fading. Each card also shows the stock's own record over the last 60 sessions, and the summary at the top shows the pooled record recomputed on every scan.

Falling stocks (Direction ▼). The same six rules run on the mirror image: ATR below the open, sigma below VWAP, through yesterday's S1 / S2 / S3, 5-minute RSI below 30, a volume climax, and the share of the fall already recovered from the low. Tested the same way on the same 60 days (NIFTY 100 + mid/small caps): after the score first crossed 35 the stock closed higher only about 47% of the time; stocks that had already recovered half the fall closed higher about 52%; very low RSI and heavy volume did no better, and none of these held up across both halves of the sample. Big drops before 11:00 leaned the other way (about 43% closed higher, average -0.4% further) but the two halves disagreed. So this list shows who is falling hard today; it does not say they will bounce.

Each card's score (0-100) adds up ten published overbought and exhaustion rules computed on daily bars. A rule contributes nothing until it triggers and its full weight at an extreme reading, so a 70 means several rules are near their extremes at once. Stretched = 50 and above, extended = 35-49, warming up = 20-34; the tiers were set from two years of NIFTY 100 daily bars so that a score of 20+ appears on about six names a day, 35+ on two and 50+ on fewer than one.

Being stretched is a statement about the next few sessions, not the trend: the momentum research (Jegadeesh & Titman 1993; George & Hwang 2004) finds that strong stocks keep going for months, while the short-term work (Connors, Bollinger) finds that the sessions right after an extreme tend to give some of it back. So every card also shows the stock's own record: each time in the last two years its score crossed 35, was it lower five sessions later, by how much on average, and how deep was the typical dip within ten sessions - next to the unconditional base rate (how often any bar of that stock closed lower five sessions later). A record clearly above the base rate means the stock has actually pulled back after such runs; a record below it means it has tended to keep running, and the stretch alone is not a reason to sell it or short it.
